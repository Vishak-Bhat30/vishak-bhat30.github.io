Vishak K Bhat

Research Fellow · Microsoft Research India

I work on making LLM reasoning reliable and efficient. I study how to verify a model's intermediate reasoning at test time and steer it toward correct, policy-compliant behavior, without paying for many sampled trajectories. I built interwhen NeurIPS 2026, a framework that runs verifiers asynchronously alongside a single reasoning trajectory and intervenes only when something goes wrong.

My research is mentored by Amit Sharma, Nagarajan Natarajan, Vineeth N. Balasubramanian, and Subbarao Kambhampati. Earlier, I worked on LLM-driven causal discovery at Sony Research India and on graph neural networks for particle physics with ML4SCI / CERN as a Google Summer of Code contributor. I graduated from IIT (ISM) Dhanbad in 2026 with an Integrated M.Tech. in Mathematics & Computing (rank 3).

I aspire to pursue a Ph.D. on LLM reasoning, computer vision, causal discovery, and multimodal learning.

interwhen: A Generalizable Framework for Steering Reasoning Models with Test-time Verification

Vishak K Bhat, Prateek Chanda, Vijval Ekbote, Ashmit Khandelwal, Maitreyi Swaroop, Vineeth N. Balasubramanian, Subbarao Kambhampati, Nagarajan Natarajan, Amit Sharma

Reasoning models make mistakes mid-way, yet most verifiers either check only the final answer or branch into many costly trajectories. interwhen watches a single trajectory. It periodically forks the model to extract verifiable state and runs code or formal verifiers asynchronously, so correct steps never wait. It steers the model with feedback only when a check fails. Verifiers can be synthesized automatically from natural-language policies, including provably correct ones in Lean and z3.

  • 32% → 87% task completion for Qwen3-30B on τ²-bench telecom, with no finetuning
  • Near-perfect accuracy on logic and spatial tasks at a fraction of Best-of-N or Tree-of-Thoughts tokens
  • No added latency on correct steps: verification runs in parallel with generation

Genesis: Towards Explainable Causal Discovery

Vishak K Bhat, Abhinav Thorat, Ravi Kumar Kolla, Niranjan Pedanekar

Statistical causal discovery leaves many edge directions ambiguous, while LLM-only discovery is hard to trust. Genesis runs Partial Ancestral Graphs (PAGs) in parallel with LLM-guided search, so each ambiguous edge is resolved with an explicit, human-readable reason. The result is a causal graph you can audit. It builds on CausalOrder and LLM-CD and is benchmarked on Asia, Cancer, Earthquake, Child, and Survey.

GNN for Muon Particle Momentum Estimation

Vishak K Bhat, Eric A. F. Reinhardt, Sergei Gleyzer

The CMS experiment at the LHC must decide quickly which collisions to keep, and better muon momentum estimates mean fewer false triggers. We turn muon-station hits into graphs using two construction methods and apply GNNs with a custom message-passing layer. The GNN beats TabNet in MAE, and we show that node-feature dimension is key to efficiency.

  • −14% MSE vs. a Transformer baseline (one architecture)
  • 27% faster inference (the other architecture)

The figure is an illustration of the pipeline, not real detector data.
